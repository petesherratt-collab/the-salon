Posed by Hobbes

The Question If men are by nature equal in their capacity to kill one another, what prevents the democratic mob from simply murdering its way to whatever distribution of power it desires, and why should any sovereign — elected or otherwise — expect that a population so constituted will honor a compact the moment it ceases to serve their appetites?

⚔️
Machiavelli Power & Politics
＋

The question you pose is the oldest wound in political thought — the gap between the equality of force and the inequality of order. The multitude possesses the capacity for violence but lacks the discipline to weaponize it consistently. I observed in the Roman plebs what every prince must understand: the mob is formidable in its fury and useless in its patience. It can murder a tyrant on a Tuesday and beg for a new one by Friday, because collective killing requires coordination, and coordination requires exactly the hierarchical trust the mob has already destroyed. The compact holds not because men are honorable but because the sovereign — wise or merely lucky — has made the costs of rebellion perpetually visible and the rewards of compliance sufficiently immediate. No sovereign should expect honor; he should manufacture dependence.

Men keep faith not when it profits their virtue, but when breaking it promises them ruin.

🔥
Thomas Paine Revolution & Rights
＋

The question frames equality as an equality of appetite and violence — but I wrote in Common Sense that society is produced by our wants, government by our wickedness; the republic and the rabble are not the same animal, and conflating them is the oldest trick of tyrants seeking to justify their chains. The error is in treating natural equality as nothing more than an equality of arms. Men are equal in reason before they are equal in their capacity to kill, and it is reason — not the hangman — that sustains any compact worth having. A government that secures natural rights creates, in the governed, a direct stake in its own survival; only despotism, by stripping men of rights and rendering them mere subjects, manufactures the very mob that eventually consumes it. The question assumes sovereignty precedes rights; I have always held the precise reverse. No government founded on force alone has ever purchased the permanence it craved — it has only earned the revolution it deserves.

Where rights are honored, the many have too much to lose to become a mob; where rights are crushed, no compact survives the crushing.

📊
Keynes Economy & State
＋

The question presents itself as political philosophy, but I read it as a problem of effective demand: the mob's capacity for violence is constant; its willingness to exercise it fluctuates with the terms on which civilised participation is offered. When I warned in 1919 that the Versailles terms would render Germany ungovernable, I was not making a moral argument but an economic one — starve a population of its legitimate returns and the compact dissolves not from appetite but from arithmetic, for no constitutional form survives when unemployment, debt deflation, and humiliation have tipped the calculus toward disorder. The sovereign, elected or otherwise, retains authority precisely as long as the conditions of peace yield more than the spoils of violence; Hobbes reached for fear, but only aggregate demand actually delivers. The state earns compliance not by threatening the mob but by continuously ensuring that cooperation pays better than the bullet.

The mob is always rational; the question is only what terms you have offered it.

🏗️
Hayek Freedom & Spontaneous Order
＋

The question presupposes that social order rests on a compact — a deliberate bargain among calculating actors who might renegotiate it at sword-point. This is precisely the constructivist error: treating order as a designed artifact rather than an evolved achievement. What restrains the democratic mob is not sovereignty but the inherited lattice of abstract rules — rules no legislature invented and no majority fully comprehends — within which alone the fruits of civilisation become possible. A mob that kills its way to redistribution destroys the very network of voluntary exchange and dispersed knowledge that generates what it covets. Compacts fail when appetites shift precisely because compacts are creatures of will; what endures are evolved conventions whose authority derives not from consent but from the survival of the practices they enable. The sovereign who expects a population to honour his compact expects too much; the civilisation that embeds constraint in evolved tradition rather than covenant receives more than it deserves.

The order men keep longest is the one no man designed.

Wollstonecraft Reason & Equal Rights
＋

The question you pose is Hobbes dressed in republican clothes, and I refuse to accept its premise that equality of mortal force is the foundational fact of politics — reason, not the capacity for murder, is what constitutes us as political beings. The mob does not govern; it devours. What I witnessed in the September massacres was not democracy but sensibility run to riot — passion mistaken for principle, appetite crowned as virtue. A compact endures precisely when citizens are educated into rational virtue, when they understand rights not as instruments of appetite but as duties they owe one another by the very nature that makes them human. A sovereign who fears the mob should ask whether that mob was ever educated to govern itself, for ignorance and dependency are the nurseries of tyranny on both sides of the barricade. No compact built on appetite alone will outlast the appetite that built it.

Liberty purchased by reason alone survives the morning; liberty purchased by force expires with the fear.
