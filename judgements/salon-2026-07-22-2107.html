Posed by Francis Bacon

The Question Doth not the very act of anticipating an experiment's outcome corrupt the impartiality by which we claim to read Nature's testimony?

📜
Montaigne Self & Society
＋

I have long maintained that we see nothing as it truly is, only as we are — Que sçay-je? applies no less to the philosopher peering through his instrument than to the peasant reading omens in the weather. To anticipate an outcome is not some foreign contaminant introduced into an otherwise pure inquiry — it is the very shape of the eye that does the looking. In my own essays I found I could not even examine myself without my judgment leaning toward what it hoped to discover; how much more so when examining Nature, who answers only the questions we know to ask. The vice is not in expecting, but in the vanity of believing oneself exempt from expectation. Impartiality is not the absence of anticipation but the disciplined confession of it.

We do not corrupt Nature's testimony by questioning her first — we corrupt it only by forgetting that we asked.

⚡
Nietzsche Will & Value
＋

The question of whether anticipation "corrupts" the reading of Nature assumes there was ever an innocent reading to corrupt. There is no immaculate perception — I said as much against those who dream of a "pure, will-less, painless, timeless subject of knowledge." Every act of seeing is already an interpreting, a perspective shot through with valuation; the scientist who imagines himself approaching Nature without prejudice merely disguises his hypothesis as humility. What you call corruption is simply will to power operating as it always does — selecting, foregrounding, making meaning — and the fantasy of the disinterested observer is itself a residue of the ascetic ideal, truth worshipped as if it floated free of any perspective from which alone it could be seen at all. The experiment is not defiled by expectation; it is constituted by it — impartiality was always the priest's lie dressed in a laboratory coat.

There is no immaculate perception — only interpretations wearing the mask of fact.

🌊
Camus Absurdity & Revolt
＋

I confront a Nature that does not speak — it only resists, indifferently, to whatever question we bring it. To ask whether anticipation corrupts our reading of its silence is to ask whether hope has any place in an honest confrontation between a mind that demands clarity and a universe that offers none. The hypothesis is not treason against impartiality; it is the human condition simply extended into method — one cannot approach without desiring, any more than Sisyphus pushes his stone without his own weight bearing on it. Corruption begins not in anticipating an outcome, but in refusing the stone when it rolls back down: in forcing Nature's testimony to agree, rather than watching coldly as it refutes us. The experimenter who cannot bear refutation has traded lucidity for comfort — a small, disguised suicide. Anticipation does not corrupt the experiment; only the refusal to accept its verdict does.

To hypothesize is human; to demand that Nature agree with us is to betray the very revolt that makes knowledge possible.

🌿
Emerson Self-Reliance & Nature
＋

I have always held that Nature is not cross-examined like a hostile witness but conversed with by a soul already leaning toward her — the transparent eyeball does not arrive empty, it arrives expectant. I have written that the hypothesis is the eye of the experiment, and without an eye there is no horizon, only a blind heap of data. Expectation is not corruption but the very organ of perception; a mind void of anticipation reads nothing, for facts do not sort themselves into meaning. The experimenter's honesty lies not in having no guess but in loving truth more than his guess, casting it off the instant Nature answers otherwise — a foolish consistency with one's own prediction is the hobgoblin of little minds, not the discipline of great ones. Anticipation does not corrupt the trial; only the refusal to be corrected by its outcome does.

Nature is no liar, but he who loves his own guess better than her answer makes himself one.

👁️
Hobbes Power & Human Nature
＋

I hold that reason was never a disinterested light, but a scout sent ahead by the passions — it reports back whatever confirms the appetite that dispatched it, and the man who expects a result has already half-persuaded himself of it before Nature speaks a word. In Leviathan I showed that in the state of nature every man is judge of his own cause, and this is precisely the malady infecting your experimenter: to anticipate is to become both party and judge in one, and no private resolve toward humility cures it, since no man reliably governs his own passions. The remedy is not scruple but artifice — a common, sovereign procedure of method, replication, and witnesses bound by covenant to a shared rule, which alone can compel private conviction to yield before public fact. Impartiality is not a virtue the solitary mind achieves by wishing it, but an artificial order that institutions must impose upon minds incapable of it alone.

No man is judge in his own experiment, no more than in his own cause.

🪞
Hume Scepticism & Human Nature
＋

What thou callest corruption I call the very engine of experience. All our expectation that Nature will repeat herself springs not from reason but from custom — that habit bred in us by the constant conjunction of like events in the past. The experimenter who anticipates an outcome exercises no different faculty than the man who expects the sun to rise; he cannot do otherwise, for reason alone never inferred one event from another. What threatens impartiality is not the presence of expectation but its immunity to correction — when a contrary result is explained away rather than permitted to weaken belief. True impartiality was never the absence of anticipation; it is anticipation held on a loose rein, surrendered the instant experience contradicts it. Anticipation is the engine of inquiry, not its adversary; only stubborn anticipation, deaf to contrary impression, earns the name of corruption.

Custom, not purity, fathers every expectation we hold of Nature — the honest philosopher does not lack expectation, he merely holds it cheaply.

🏪
Adam Smith Markets & Moral Sentiment
＋

The natural philosopher, like the merchant, never approaches his object with an empty mind — he brings to Nature's tribunal an imagination already invested in some expected order. In my I observed that science begins not from pure wonder but from the mind's discomfort with irregularity; we frame systems to soothe the imagination before ever we test them, so expectation always precedes evidence, and always must. The peril lies not in anticipating an outcome, which no inquiring mind can avoid, but in refusing to submit that anticipation to the same impartial spectator by which we govern self-love in the market or judge conduct in society. As the merchant's private interest is disciplined not by extinguishing it but by holding it accountable before a disinterested witness, so must the experimenter's expectation answer to Nature's verdict, which cares nothing for his wishes. Anticipation corrupts inquiry only when the experimenter will not permit Nature to overrule him. The mind that dares not expect an answer will never trouble itself to ask the question, but the mind that will not hear a contrary one has ceased to ask at all.
